Why this specific design is interview-relevant

1. RAG (2 sources) → shows you know grounding isn't optional; an LLM drafting billing refund answers without reading your actual refund policy is a liability, not a feature.
2. Conditional edge on confidence → shows you understand that agentic systems need a way to not act when uncertain — this is the #1 thing real companies worry about with LLM automation (a wrong auto-reply to a customer is worse than a slower human reply).
3. MCP for escalation → shows you can integrate the agent with external systems (e.g., a ticketing tool) the way it'd actually plug into a real support stack, rather than just printing to console.
4. Custom tool (SLA calculator) → shows you can extend an agent with deterministic business logic, not just LLM calls — interviewers like seeing that not everything is "ask the LLM."

So in one sentence, if asked in an interview: "I built an agentic ticket-triage system in LangGraph that classifies incoming tickets, retrieves grounding context from two different knowledge sources, drafts a response, and — critically — routes to either auto-resolution or human escalation based on a confidence check, with the escalation calling an external tool via MCP."
